# Hooks: the first three seconds of a UGC video

Read this with `copy.md` before proposing ideas in `crear-video`. A hook decides whether the rest
of the video exists, and *different* hooks reach different buyers, so the three ideas should not
be three rewordings of one. Adapted from `ad-creative` (hook system, short-form video specs); see
`README.md`. Lines are Rioplatense Spanish; they must pass the gate in `copy.md` and come only
from the allowed claims in `ficha.md`.

## 1. A hook has up to three components

| Component | What it is | In the studio |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | What happens on screen in seconds 0-3 | Always. It stops the thumb. |
| **Spoken line** | The first words the actor says | Always. It opens the loop. |
| **On-screen text** | Header or overlay | **Not for video.** Seedance prompts say no subtitles and no on-screen text. Static ads (`crear-creativos`) use visual + headline instead. |

For video the hook is visual + spoken line, and they **complement, never repeat**. If she says
"las patas se giran con la mano", the camera must not also be a still talking head: show the hand
turning the leg and let the line say why it matters. Write both when you propose an idea
("Visual: … / Dice: …"); a hook with only the line is half a hook.

Every hook needs a source: the buyer's own words (questions from `meli_search_questions`, reviews
when available, `ficha.md` evidence) and the allowed claim it relies on. Customer wording beats
marketing paraphrase.

## 2. Build it in order

Segment (a slice of buyers with a shared situation) → motivation (the one pain, desire or doubt,
in their words) → format (section 5) → hook. Pick the format *before* the line: the same pain
reads differently as a reaction than as a confession.

## 3. Opening moves

Cycle through them; do not default to the same one. One Rioplatense example each, short enough to
pronounce (the product is illustrative).

| Move | How it works | Example line | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity gap** | Withhold the key noun, then pay it off in the video | "Esto es lo que hacía que se me mueva la cama." | Must be answered within the video, or it is clickbait. |
| **Confession** | Admit a common mistake | "Yo las ponía mal y me costó darme cuenta." | Only with a visible, concrete mistake; no invented personal history (see `copy.md`). |
| **POV / relatable** | Mirror a very specific situation | "Cuando te sentás en la cama y se mueve todo." | Specific or invisible; generic POV does nothing. |
| **Proof-first** | Open on the result or the money shot | (Visual: la cama firme.) "Mirá cómo quedó." | Only proof the listing or the footage can show. |
| **Contrast** | Two real options or two states in the first beat | "¿Las compro lisas o con base ancha? Te muestro la diferencia." | Compare two things the buyer really weighs, never with a named competitor. |
| **Question** | The exact question buyers ask | "¿Esto entra en cualquier sommier?" | Use the buyer's phrasing; answer with the softer allowed claim, not "cualquiera". |
| **Bold, falsifiable claim** | A specific statement the video then shows | "Se ajustan con la mano, mirá." | The claim must be on the allowed list. |
| **Direct question to a doubt** | Voice the objection first | "¿Será difícil de armar? Fijate." | The body must actually answer it. |

Contrast hooks are the source of the "no es X, es Y" tell: use contrast when the buyer truly
compares two things, not to deny something nobody said. Avoid personal-attribute wording
("¿Tenés…?") per `meta-ads.md`.

## 4. The on-ramp rule

Seconds ~3-15 must continue the premise of the hook. If the hook promises the reason the bed
moved, the next beat explains that reason; it does not jump to a brand pitch. Corollary: when two
ideas share a body but have different hooks, rewrite the on-ramp for each. Judge hold rate
separately from thumbstop.

## 5. UGC formats a synthetic actor can do

The actor is generated, so only formats that need no real person, credential or screen capture:

| Format | Shape | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Reaction + demo** | Short reaction line, hard cut to the product being used | Anything with a visible, satisfying action. Default workhorse. |
| **Yapper / talking head** | One person, one room, talks to the lens and lands on the product | Products needing a little explanation; keep the arc tight (hook → problem → product as the turn). |
| **Problem → solution** | Show the annoyance, then the product fixing it | Clear daily problem with a visible fix (check the physical-setup limits in `crear-video`). |
| **Demo / tutorial** | Hands and steps, short lines | Assembly, use or fit questions buyers ask a lot. |
| **Unboxing / first look** | Open the package, show what comes in | Giftable or "what do I get" products; claim only what the listing says is included. |
| **Choice / comparison of own options** | Two variants, sizes or uses of the client's product | When the buyer hesitates between variants. |

Leave out formats that need real people, credentials or other tools: authority or expert
endorsements, founder stories and vlogs, VSLs, street interviews, duets with another creator's
footage, screen recordings, code-rendered video and CapCut-style editing recipes. Do not claim
real-world capture the actor did not do.

Vary one dimension across the three ideas (hook, angle or format), not all at once, so the
result teaches something.

## 6. Reading results (briefly)

| Stage | Metric | Weak means | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop | Thumbstop / 3 s views | The visual opening | New opening, same everything else |
| Stay | Hold rate | The on-ramp | Rework seconds 3-15, not the hook |
| Click | CTR | Offer or proof clarity | Sharper promise or proof |
| Convert | CVR after the click | The listing does not continue the ad | Fix the claim or the listing |

A great thumbstop is not a great ad: high stop with a collapsed hold or CVR is clickbait. Change
one component per iteration. How to read the real numbers by `adName` belongs to the
`leer-resultados` skill; here this table only explains what each metric says about the hook.
